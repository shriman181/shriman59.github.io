Process

From raw data
to field intelligence

Five stages. Each built for the constraints and sensitivities of professional archaeological and museum workflows.

01
Data Ingestion
Upload LiDAR point clouds, aerial imagery, drone surveys, or archival photography via secure API or web interface.
02
Preprocessing & Normalisation
Relief visualisation toolbox converts raw ALS data into ML-friendly formats. Noise filtering corrects for vegetation and signal decay.
03
AI Analysis
Multi-model ensemble — segmentation, object detection, and classification networks — runs in parallel, with confidence scoring on every output.
04
Expert Validation Layer
Archaeologist-in-the-loop review interface flags ambiguous detections for human confirmation before results are finalised and exported.
05
Deliverable Export
GIS-ready shapefiles, 3D mesh exports, provenance reports, and API access to structured findings — formatted to museum and government standards.
Field Applications

Built for the institutions

Southeast Asia · UNESCO World Heritage

Detecting Angkor Wat's Hidden Urban Network

Blissora's LiDAR pipeline processed 370 km² of Cambodian jungle canopy, identifying previously unknown hydraulic infrastructure and residential districts of the Khmer Empire buried beneath dense vegetation — extending the known urban footprint by 40%.

Result
Survey completed in 6 weeks vs. 18+ months of manual field mapping. 47 new structures identified for excavation priority.
Middle East · Post-Conflict Heritage

3D Reconstruction of Palmyra's Damaged Temples

Using pre-conflict aerial photography, drone surveys, and historical photogrammetry archives, Blissora generated precise 3D digital twins of Palmyra structures destroyed between 2015 and 2017 — enabling reconstruction planning and cultural memory preservation.

Result
Sub-centimetre accuracy reconstruction models delivered to Syrian Ministry of Culture and UNESCO for ongoing restoration program.
Global · Art Market Integrity

Provenance Audit for Major Auction House Collection

Knowledge graph analysis of 1,400 antiquities consigned for auction cross-referenced 60+ international databases, academic publications, and trafficking watchlists — surfacing 23 objects with gaps in post-1970 UNESCO Convention ownership history.

Result
Flagged objects withdrawn pending investigation. Auction house averted significant legal and reputational exposure.
Central America · Pre-Columbian Sites

Maya Ceremonial Complex Discovery in Guatemala

Drone-mounted LiDAR over 200 km² of Petén jungle revealed a complex of 12 previously undocumented Maya ceremonial structures with causeways, reservoirs, and fortifications — rewriting the known settlement hierarchy of the Late Classic period.

Result
Published in peer-reviewed journal. Ministry of Culture designating site for protected status. Excavation licensed to UNAM.

The Founder
PG

Pranav Gandhi

Founder & CEO, Blissora

Pranav Gandhi founded Blissora at the intersection of two worlds he refused to see as separate: the rigour of machine learning systems and the irreplaceable weight of human cultural memory. He spent years watching field archaeologists drown in raw LiDAR data — petabytes of signal, parsed by eye and intuition — while museum curators ran provenance searches on spreadsheets.

His conviction: the tools to fix this already existed. They just hadn't been assembled into a platform that understood what archaeologists and heritage institutions actually needed. Blissora is that platform — built not to replace expert judgment, but to give it tenfold leverage.

"Every structure we fail to find before a bulldozer or a looter reaches it is a question humanity loses the right to ask forever. The data is there. We just needed to build the eyes to read it."
— Pranav Gandhi, Founder
